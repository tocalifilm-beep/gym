Thursday — Run

Cardio · ~45 min

The week’s only run. Easy for the first four weeks, then intervals.

3 km
Distance
30 min

How the session runs

  • 5 min brisk walk to warm up — never start cold at night.
  • 3 km at a conversational pace. You should be able to speak a full sentence without gasping. If you can't, slow down or walk 60s.
  • 5 min walk to cool down, then 5 min of stretching: calves, quads, hamstrings, hip flexors.

Run/walk is a legitimate way to start

If 3 km straight is too much right now, do 3 min jog / 2 min walk × 6. Same 30 minutes, same distance, far less joint stress. Drop one walk interval every couple of weeks until it's continuous.

Where to take it next

Run it easy for the first 4 weeks. Once 3 km in 30 min feels comfortable, switch to intervals: after the warm-up, 1 min faster / 2 min easy × 8, then walk to finish. When the intervals feel easy too, add distance — 4 km — rather than more speed.

Placement matters. Thursday sits two days after Tuesday’s legs and two days before Saturday’s, so you never run on freshly trained legs or lift legs after a hard run.

Sunday — Rest

Recovery

This is not a day off from the plan. It's the day the plan works.

What actually helps

  • Walk 30-45 min at an easy pace, ideally outdoors. Recovery, not training.
  • Sleep in. With six 10 PM finishes, this is your one chance to catch up.
  • Cook for the week. Fat loss is decided in the kitchen far more than in the gym.
  • 10 min of stretching on whatever felt tightest during the week.

How it works

Read once

Five things that decide whether this plan works over the next three months.

How to add weight (the only progression rule you need)

It's called double progression, and it's simple:

  • Pick a weight you can do for the bottom of the rep range with 2-3 reps left in the tank.
  • Stay at that weight until you hit the top of the range on every set with clean form.
  • Then add the smallest increment available — 2.5 kg on machines and barbells, one dumbbell size up — and start again at the bottom of the range.

Write the weight in the log field next to each exercise so you're not guessing next week. That single habit is worth more than any exercise choice on this page.

Weekly volume — why it's built this way

Each muscle gets roughly 10-16 hard sets per week, which is where beginners see the most growth without needing more recovery than a 6-day schedule allows.

  • Chest: 10 sets (Mon)
  • Back: 14 sets (Wed)
  • Shoulders: 11 sets (Fri) + 3 sets of face pulls (Wed)
  • Glutes & hamstrings: 14 sets (Tue)
  • Quads: 12 sets (Sat) + 2 optional sets of adductors
  • Calves: 3 sets (Tue)
  • Biceps: 8 (Wed, Fri) · Triceps: 9 (Mon, Fri)

Legs are trained twice a week, four days apart: the back of the legs on Tuesday, the front on Saturday. No muscle group gets hit hard on back-to-back days. If you have to skip a day, skip the run, not a lifting day.

Muscle + fat loss at the same time

Doing both at once is realistic for beginners, and this is roughly the setup that makes it possible:

  • Protein is the lever. Aim for about 1.6-2.2 g per kg of bodyweight per day. This is the single biggest difference between losing fat and losing muscle.
  • A small calorie deficit, not a large one. Aggressive cuts kill your gym performance and take the muscle with the fat.
  • Sleep 7-8 hours. With a 10 PM finish this is the hard part, which is why it's on the list.
  • Judge progress by the mirror, the tape measure and your logged weights — not the scale. The scale will lie to you when muscle goes up and fat goes down at the same time.

I'm not a nutritionist or a doctor. If either of you has an existing injury, a medical condition, or is pregnant or planning to be, get this looked at by a professional who can actually see you.

Training at 8:30 PM
  • Eat a real meal 2-3 hours before (around 6 PM) — not right beforehand.
  • Keep caffeine before 5 PM. A pre-workout at 8 PM will cost you sleep, and sleep is where the muscle actually gets built.
  • Give yourself 30-45 min to wind down after getting home. Lights low, screen brightness down.
  • Late training is fine for performance — you're typically strongest in the evening. It's only sleep you need to protect.
Two people, one plan

Both people run the exact same sessions — same exercises, same sets, same rep ranges. Only the loads differ, and those are individual.

Practical notes for training together:

  • Alternate sets on the same machine. One works, one rests — you both get proper rest and the session doesn't run past 10 PM.
  • Keep separate logs. Every exercise has two fields, one per person. Two sets of numbers, two progressions.
  • Upper-body pulling usually progresses slower for women, since a larger share of muscle mass sits in the lower body. Pull-ups may take months rather than weeks — that's a normal starting point, not a plateau.
  • Higher reps often feel better on rows and pulldowns for whoever is weaker at upper-body pulling. 12-15 reps instead of 10-12 is a fine adjustment; the growth stimulus is comparable.

When to take an easy week

Every 6-8 weeks, or any time you feel run down: keep the same exercises, cut the sets roughly in half and use about 70% of your usual weight. Keep the Thursday run easy — no intervals. One week of this and you come back stronger — pushing through instead is how beginners stall or get hurt.

Signs it's time: your logged weights go down two weeks running, your sleep gets worse, or the warm-up starts feeling like the workout.
